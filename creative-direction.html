A brand system that was complete on paper — but had no voice in the world.

Optimite had an established brand system — colour palette, typography, logo. But there was no cohesive visual language translating that system into social content and platform-specific strategy. The social presence lacked personality and consistency.

As Head of Design, I led creative direction and visual system design across all social platforms, working closely with the Head of Content. Rather than starting from scratch, I inherited the existing brand and focused on translating it into a living, recognisable character — one that would resonate across platforms and become a genuine client touchpoint.

Leadership takeaways

On building a brand that lives in the world.

1
A brand system becomes real through a living character
By inheriting an existing identity and focusing on how to bring it to life socially, I was able to create immediate recognition and consistency without starting from zero. The mascot gave everyone — team and clients — something to rally around.
2
AI-assisted design accelerates iteration, not replaces direction
Using generative tools to explore concepts quickly, then refining them through deliberate creative direction — that's the real value. The creative judgement is still human. The speed is what changes.
3
Scaling creative vision requires systems thinking
Leading visual direction across multiple platforms taught me how to scale through systems, cross-functional collaboration, and platform-specific adaptation — not by doing everything yourself, but by building structures others can follow.
